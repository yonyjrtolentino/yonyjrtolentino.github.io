Help center

Everything you need to go live with overlays from your phone: stream keys step by step, frequently asked questions and support.

Before you start

  • A stable upload connection: about 5 Mbps for 720p, 8 Mbps for 1080p30 and 10–12 Mbps for 1080p60 (check it with a speed test). 5 GHz Wi-Fi or 5G works best.
  • Keep the phone plugged into a 20 W (or stronger) charger for long streams.
  • Take the case off and keep it out of the sun, so quality doesn't drop because of heat.
  • An account on the platform with live streaming enabled (see each platform's guide).
  • To stream to several platforms at once, the upload you need adds up (two destinations at 6 Mbps = 12 Mbps).

Twitch

Free, no follower minimum. The key doesn't change.

What you need

  • A Twitch account (free). There is no follower minimum to stream.
  • Recommended: turn on two-factor authentication in Twitch › Settings › Security and Privacy. Twitch requires it for some features and it protects your key.
  • At least 8 Mbps upload for 1080p60 (or 5 Mbps for 720p60).

Step by step

  1. Tap “Open the Twitch dashboard” (below) and sign in.
  2. Go to Settings › Stream.
  3. Under “Primary Stream key”, tap “Copy”.
  4. Go back to Yoni's Stream: the app detects the copied key and offers to put it in your Twitch destination.
  5. Check that the Twitch destination is on and tap LIVE. The server address is already filled in.

Direct links

  • Open the Twitch dashboard (key)
  • Turn on two-factor authentication

Tips

  • Twitch accepts up to 1080p at 60 fps and 6 Mbps: use 6000 kbps bitrate at 1080p60 or 4500 kbps at 720p60.
  • Never show your key on screen. If someone sees it, tap “Reset” on the same page.
  • Twitch chat connects with just your channel name (Settings › Chat and panels).

YouTube

Verify your channel; the first time can take up to 24 hours.

What you need

  • To be at least 16 years old.
  • To verify your channel with your phone number (once).
  • No live streaming restrictions in the last 90 days.
  • The first time you enable live streaming, YouTube can take up to 24 hours. After that it's instant.
  • With a streaming app like this one (an encoder) there is no subscriber minimum.

Step by step

  1. Tap “Verify my channel” (below) and follow the steps with your phone.
  2. Tap “Open YouTube Studio” › Create › Go live. If it's your first time, wait until it's enabled (up to 24 h).
  3. In the “Stream” tab, copy the “Stream key”.
  4. Go back to Yoni's Stream and paste it into your YouTube destination. The address rtmp://a.rtmp.youtube.com/live2 is already filled in.
  5. Tap LIVE here. YouTube shows your stream within seconds; if you turned off auto-start, press “Go live” in YouTube Studio.

Direct links

  • Verify my channel
  • Open YouTube Studio
  • YouTube's live streaming requirements

Tips

  • The key stays the same between streams: you only copy it once.
  • YouTube accepts up to 4K at 60 fps. For 1080p60, use 9000–12000 kbps if your connection allows it.
  • Dual stream: go live in landscape with one key and in portrait (Shorts) with another, using the app's vertical canvas.
  • To reply to chat faster, choose “Low” or “Ultra-low” latency in the stream settings in YouTube Studio.

Kick

Copy the URL and the key from the creator dashboard.

What you need

  • A Kick account with a verified email.
  • If Kick asks for it to show the key, turn on two-factor authentication in your profile › Settings › Security.
  • At least 8 Mbps upload for 1080p60.

Step by step

  1. Tap “Open the Kick dashboard” (below) and sign in.
  2. In the creator dashboard go to Settings › Stream.
  3. Copy the “Stream URL” (it starts with rtmps://) and paste it as the URL of your Kick destination.
  4. Copy the “Stream Key” and paste it as the destination's key.
  5. Tap LIVE.

Direct links

  • Open the Kick dashboard (URL and key)

Tips

  • Kick works well at 1080p60 with a 6000–8000 kbps bitrate.
  • Kick chat connects with your channel name (Settings › Chat and panels).

TikTok LIVE

18+ and usually 1,000 followers. The key changes every stream.

What you need

  • To be 18 or older.
  • Usually at least 1,000 followers (it depends on your country and account).
  • An account without recent Community Guidelines violations.
  • TikTok decides who gets a stream key (server URL and key). If you don't see it, your account doesn't have access yet: it doesn't depend on this app.

Step by step

  1. Tap “Open TikTok LIVE Producer” (below). If it opens the app, open it in Chrome and turn on ⋮ › “Desktop site”.
  2. Sign in, choose a category (for example, Gaming) and type the stream title.
  3. Tap “Save and go LIVE”. The “Server URL” and the “Stream key” appear below.
  4. Copy the URL and paste it into your TikTok destination; then copy the key and paste it too.
  5. In Yoni's Stream use the vertical canvas (9:16) for TikTok and tap LIVE.

Direct links

  • Open TikTok LIVE Producer
  • TikTok LIVE Center

Tips

  • The key changes every stream and when you sign out: copy it again before each stream.
  • TikTok recommends vertical 1080×1920. At 720×1280, 30 fps and 3000–4000 kbps the stream is steadier on mobile data.
  • TikTok chat is read from its website (experimental): set it in Settings › Chat and panels.

Instagram Live

Professional, public account. The key changes every stream.

What you need

  • A professional (creator or business), public account. Personal accounts don't see the key.
  • Instagram usually requires at least 1,000 followers to stream with an external app.
  • It's done from instagram.com with the browser in desktop mode.

Step by step

  1. Tap “Open Instagram in the browser” (below). In Chrome turn on ⋮ › “Desktop site” and sign in.
  2. Tap Create (+) › “Live video”.
  3. Type a title, choose “Public” and tap “Next”.
  4. Copy the “Stream URL” and the “Stream key” and paste them into your Instagram destination. Keep that page open.
  5. Tap LIVE in Yoni's Stream. When Instagram shows your picture, tap “Go live” in Instagram.

Direct links

  • Open Instagram in the browser

Tips

  • The key changes every stream: repeat steps 2 to 4 each time.
  • Use the vertical canvas (9:16).
  • Instagram doesn't let other apps read comments: you see them in Instagram.

Facebook Live

Account at least 60 days old; pages need 100 followers. Persistent key.

What you need

  • A Facebook account at least 60 days old.
  • To stream from a page or a profile in professional mode: at least 100 followers.
  • It's done from Live Producer in the browser (desktop mode).

Step by step

  1. Tap “Open Live Producer” (below). In Chrome turn on ⋮ › “Desktop site” and sign in.
  2. Choose where to post (your profile or your page) and the “Streaming software” option.
  3. Turn on “Use a persistent stream key” so you don't have to change it every stream.
  4. Copy the “Stream key” and paste it into your Facebook destination. The URL rtmps://live-api-s.facebook.com:443/rtmp/ is already filled in.
  5. Tap LIVE here and, when Live Producer shows your picture, press “Go live”.

Direct links

  • Open Live Producer

Tips

  • Facebook accepts 1080p at 30 or 60 fps up to about 9 Mbps.
  • Facebook comments are read in Facebook (other apps aren't allowed to read them).

X (Twitter)

From Media Studio Producer, with a “Custom” destination.

What you need

  • Access to X's Media Studio Producer. X may require a Premium subscription to stream.
  • It's done from studio.x.com with the browser in desktop mode.

Step by step

  1. Tap “Open X Media Studio” (below) and sign in.
  2. Go to Producer › Sources › “Create source”: choose RTMP and your region.
  3. Copy the source's RTMP URL and key.
  4. In Yoni's Stream add a “Custom” destination and paste the URL and the key.
  5. In Producer create a broadcast (Broadcasts › Create broadcast) with that source, tap LIVE here and then start the broadcast on X.

Direct links

  • Open X Media Studio

Tips

  • X recommends 720p or 1080p at 30 fps.

Other platforms and servers

Rumble, Trovo, your own server… over RTMP, RTMPS or SRT.

What you need

  • The server address (rtmp://, rtmps:// or srt://) and the key your platform or server gives you.

Step by step

  1. On the platform's website look for “Streaming software”, “Encoder”, “RTMP” or “Stream key”.
  2. In Yoni's Stream add a “Custom” destination.
  3. Paste the server URL and the key into their fields (with SRT, everything goes in the URL, for example srt://ip:port?streamid=...).
  4. Tap LIVE.

Tips

  • If the platform asks for a maximum bitrate, set it in Settings › Video.

Frequently asked questions

The stream drops or stutters

Lower the bitrate or switch to 720p in Settings › Video, move closer to the router or use 5 GHz Wi-Fi, and close apps that are downloading things. With Deluxe and Streamer the app reconnects automatically if the connection drops.

My phone gets hot

The app acts early: when the phone starts to warm up, it first lightens only what your audience doesn't see (the picture on your screen drops fps and, if you don't touch it for 2 minutes, dims; touching it brings it back). The studio screen always runs at 60 Hz and a still scene stops being redrawn until something changes. If the phone still heats up, the app lowers the stream quality step by step so the stream doesn't drop, and restores it when it cools down. You can help: take the case off, use a good charger, keep it out of the sun and, if needed, turn on Efficiency mode in Settings › Performance and battery.

How does it save battery?

The studio screen runs at 60 Hz, the studio rests when nothing changes, layers are captured at 30 fps if your stream runs at 30, Wi-Fi only runs at full power if you stream over Wi-Fi, and the TikTok chat is read without animations. With Android's battery saver on, or below 30% battery while not charging, the picture on your screen gets lighter on its own; the stream stays the same. What helps most: stream with the charger plugged in and lower the brightness.

I can't hear the game sound

Android lets apps capture the sound of almost all games and apps, but some block it. In that case use a USB capture card or the Wi-Fi input.

My console doesn't show up

Follow “Connect a console step by step” in the Studio tab. The PS5 needs a DNS change; with a USB capture card any console works.

I don't get a TikTok or Instagram key

Those platforms only give a key to accounts that meet their requirements (age, followers, professional account). See their guide in “Stream keys step by step”.

How do I cancel my subscription?

In Google Play › your photo › Payments & subscriptions › Subscriptions › Yoni's Stream › Cancel. You keep the plan until the end of the period you paid for.

Where are my recordings?

In the phone's gallery, in the Movies/YonisStream folder (screenshots in Pictures/YonisStream).

I changed phones

On the old phone: Settings › Google account and backup › Back up now. On the new one: sign in with the same account and tap Restore.

The USB capture card isn't detected

Use an OTG adapter or a USB-C to USB-C cable, accept the access permission and try another cable. USB 3.0 capture cards need a USB 3 port on the phone for uncompressed 1080p60.

The capture card has no sound

The game sound comes through the same USB cable as the picture and shows up in the Audio tab as “Game · capture card”, at the same time as your microphone. On the PS5, go to Settings › Sound › Audio Output › Output to Headphones and choose “Chat Audio”: with “All Audio”, the game only goes to the console's headphones and never reaches the capture card. To hear the game yourself, connect headphones to the phone (“Hear the game in my headphones”, in Audio › Audio monitoring). If you hear nothing, raise the phone's media volume. If the stream only plays a hum, set Settings › Sound › Audio Output › Audio Format (Priority) to “Linear PCM” on the PS5.

There's a low hum in the background

Many cheap capture cards add a hum from their own power. The app detects it and removes it automatically. If you still notice it, try another OTG adapter or a USB hub with its own charger, and another HDMI cable.

The capture card gives picture without sound, or sound without picture

This happens mostly with USB 3.0 capture cards connected through a hub: uncompressed video and sound at the same time overload the phone's USB. In the capture card layer choose “Automatic” or “MJPEG” and 1080p. The app opens the picture first and, once it arrives, the sound; “Automatic” always uses MJPEG at 1080p60 with these capture cards, and the app never touches the USB sound interfaces. If it still fails, try without the hub or with a hub that has its own charger.

With the capture card, my voice isn't heard

Check three things, in this order. 1) That the mic isn't muted: if the top says “MIC MUTED” or the mic button is red and crossed out, tap it. 2) Which microphone you chose: in Audio, tap the ⚙ of “Your voice”; each option says what is connected, with its name and number. Bluetooth headphones are never used as a mic (their mic would put the phone in call mode and drop the game to 8000 Hz). 3) The other ways to add your voice, in the next question. If it still doesn't work, Audio › Show advanced settings › Audio status has the details to send to support.

It's noisy where I stream: how do I add my voice without everything being heard?

Three ways, in the ⚙ of “Your voice”: 1) “Voice built into the capture card (3.5 mm jack)”: plug a mic or a headset with a mic into the capture card and your voice arrives mixed with the game, without opening another mic. 2) Another USB device on the hub (for example, a PlayStation Link adapter): choose it in the list of inputs; if it doesn't appear, your phone only allows one USB sound device at a time. 3) The phone's microphone (the bottom one) with the “Noise filter”: it cuts rumble and wind and keeps the mic silent while you're not talking; set the threshold between -60 and -20 dB.

When I connect Bluetooth headphones, the game goes silent or sounds worse

The mic of Bluetooth headphones works in “call mode”, and on many phones that mode puts all sound in call quality (8000 Hz, mono), including the capture card's. That's why the app never uses the mic of Bluetooth headphones: they're only used for listening, in stereo and high quality, and never put the phone in call mode. If the capture card briefly arrives at 8000 or 16000 Hz when connected, the app reopens it at 48000 Hz on its own, without interruptions.

The game sounds too quiet on stream

The game comes in at 60% and only drops a little while you talk, and the whole stream sounds loud and even (about −16 LUFS, like other streams). You can adjust it in Audio › Your voice and the game: “Recommended (game 60%)” keeps it as it comes, and “Clear voice (game 30%)” puts your voice further ahead.

The music in my room can be heard on stream

The automatic volume of your voice only opens the mic with sounds close to your voice level: music, the TV or background noise don't open it when you're quiet. While you talk, whatever plays nearby is heard under your voice, so turn the room music off for the stream (also, copyrighted music can get your streams muted or removed). For a noisy room, turn on the “Noise filter” in the ⚙ of “Your voice”.

I want to hear exactly what my audience hears

In Audio › Audio monitoring, turn on “Monitor the stream as it is” with headphones connected to the phone: you'll hear the stream exactly as it goes out. Each source also has its own monitoring in its ⚙: Off, Monitor only, or Monitor and output.

My voice chat friends don't come through on stream

With headphones connected to the PS5, the console sends voice chat (Discord or party) only to those headphones and not over HDMI, so it never reaches the capture card. To get your friends on stream, disconnect the headphones from the PS5, talk with the controller's mic and listen to everything (game and friends) with headphones connected to the phone.
